The national scholarship application, step by step — what to prepare, when to move, and what happens if you don't get it.

The application,

Door one: the national scholarship

Full tuition, stipend, books. You apply during your senior year of high school (the application typically opens in the spring of junior year and closes in late January of senior year). Here's the sequence:

Start your application →

Eligibility requirements (ROD Section 2-1):

  • Citizenship: U.S. citizen, naturalized citizen, or have submitted naturalization papers. Scholarship selectees must hold U.S. citizenship to activate the scholarship.
  • Age: at least 17 and not yet 23 on or before September 1 of the enrollment year; under 27 at commissioning. Prior active-duty service extends the limit by months served.
  • Academics: Navy/Nurse — SAT 540 Math and 550 Evidence-Based Reading and Writing (1100 combined), ACT 21 Math and 22 English (44 combined), or AFQT 81. Marine — SAT 1000 combined, ACT 22 composite, or AFQT 74. Scores may be superscored within the same test type.
  • Physical: meet the physical requirements for the NROTC program.
  • Medical: complete the DoDMERB physical examination and be found qualified.

Source: ROD Chapter 2; NSTC application guidance

The scholarship application consists of the following components:

  • Transcript & test scores
  • Applicant Fitness Assessment (AFA) — Navy/Nurse: 2-minute crunches, 2-minute push-ups, 1-mile run, administered by a PE instructor, coach, or teacher. Marine option: the PFT — max set of pull-ups, timed forearm plank, 3-mile run, administered by your recruiter.
  • Leadership & activities
  • Personal statement
  • Teacher evaluations
  • Interview — conducted by a Navy officer

The medical exam comes after you're selected, not with your application.

  • DoDMERB physical — the medical qualification exam: vision, hearing, and medical history. Your scholarship doesn't activate until you're found medically qualified.

Door two: the College Program

Didn't get the scholarship? Found NROTC after starting college? The College Program is the second door.

  • Apply directly through your university's NROTC unit — no national board
  • You participate fully: classes, drill, activities, uniforms — just no tuition money or stipend yet
  • After a strong freshman year, compete for a 3-year side-load scholarship; after sophomore year, a 2-year
  • Not selected? You're screened for Advanced Standing — finish the program, earn the stipend, commission
The honest math: plenty of officers started as College Programmers. The units notice who shows up early, scores high on the fitness test, and leads without being asked. If you want the scholarship, be undeniable.

Source: ROD §§ 2-1, 2-2, 2-10

Timeline cheat sheet — National Scholarship

WhenWhat to do
Junior year springThe application opens — the cycle starts about 18 months before the school year. Create your NETFOCUS account and start the 10-screen application. It takes several hours across multiple sessions; you can save and come back. Log in at least every 30 days or your account goes inactive.
Summer before senior yearFinish the essays — 250 to 500 words each, your own work. Line up three references: a counselor or administrator, a math/science/English teacher (math for the Navy option), and a teacher, coach, counselor, or employer. Take the fitness assessment: the Applicant Fitness Assessment for Navy/Nurse (any PE instructor, coach, or teacher can administer it) or the PFT with your recruiter for the Marine option. Sign the three required statements with your recruiter.
Senior year fallSubmit early — early applications go before the early selection boards, and packages not selected roll to the next board. Complete the officer interview: your recruiter arranges it, or schedule it yourself at the nearest NTAG or NROTC unit and bring a copy of your application. Make sure official test scores reach NSTC — your package isn't complete without qualifying scores. Pre-Med: complete package due December 31.
JanuaryDeadline: the online application must be submitted by the end of January. You can update a submitted application until midnight, January 31.
SpringFinal boards meet and decisions go out. Offers name one of your five school choices — you must be admitted to that school, and the scholarship can't move to another school without approval. Complete your DoDMERB medical exam when scheduled — the scholarship doesn't activate until you're medically qualified.
SummerAttend New Student Indoctrination: about three weeks at Recruit Training Command, Great Lakes, before freshman year.
One more thing: apply to the universities on your own timeline too. The NROTC scholarship and college admission are separate processes — you need both.

Sources: NSTC NROTC Scholarship Application Instructions (1 May 2026); NETC news releases on NSI.
