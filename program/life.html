Academics & daily life

What the four years take

The degree rules, the weekly rhythm, the fitness bars, and how the battalion runs.

Academic tiers

The Navy strongly prefers technical degrees — and the rule is stricter than most expect. No less than 85% of incoming Navy-option freshman scholarship offers must go to Tier 1 or Tier 2 majors:

TierMajors
Tier 1Aerospace, Chemical, Electrical, Mechanical, Naval Architecture & Marine/Naval, Nuclear, Ocean, and Systems Engineering — these 8 disciplines only
Tier 2Other engineering (civil, computer, biomedical, industrial, materials, petroleum), math, physics, chemistry, computer science, and other technical fields
Tier 3Liberal arts, humanities, business, and all others

Tiers apply to the Navy option only — the Marine Corps option has no major restrictions. Changing tiers needs PNS approval, and moving to Tier 3 goes before a panel; if no Tier 3 quota exists you may be moved to the College Program instead.

Source: ROD § 3-5

Degree requirements

Beyond your major, every Navy-option scholarship midshipman must complete:

  • Calculus — differential and integral calculus of one variable, at least 6 semester hours, done by the end of sophomore year
  • Physics — calculus-based (mechanics, electricity, magnetism) with labs, at least 6 semester hours, done by the end of junior year
  • English — 6 semester hours of grammar and composition with significant writing
  • American history / national security policy and world culture / regional studies — PNS-approved courses

Failing calculus or physics twice puts you on academic leave of absence. AP/CLEP credit counts only if your university awards degree credit — and it can't cover both semesters of calculus and physics. College Program students instead need one year of college math and one year of lab science.

Naval science courses (one per term, roughly in this order): Introduction to Naval Science → Sea Power and Maritime Affairs → Leadership and Management → Navigation → Evolution of Warfare → Naval Ships Systems I (Engineering) → Naval Ships Systems II (Weapons) → Fundamentals of Maneuver Warfare → Naval Operations and Seamanship → Leadership and Ethics. Plus a two-hour naval science lab every week (drill and professional training) and 20 hours of Command and Leadership Training in each of your last two years.

Source: ROD §§ 3-5, 3-6, 3-7, 3-12

Academic standards

  • 2.5 cumulative GPA and 2.5 term GPA minimum, on a 4.0 scale
  • No failing grades in any degree or commissioning requirement
  • Full-time enrollment (under 12 credits a term without PNS approval is a deficiency)
  • Stay on schedule for calculus/physics and keep your class advisor informed on degree progress

Source: ROD § 3-18

A week in the program

  • One naval science course per term (~3 credits)
  • Two-hour lab each week — close-order drill and professional training
  • Physical training with the unit, up to 3 mornings a week
  • Normal total course load: 15–18 semester hours per term, NROTC included

Source: ROD §§ 3-5, 3-7, 3-17

Fitness standards

Navy option
  • Physical Fitness Assessment: forearm plank, push-ups, 1.5-mile run — twice a year
  • Must score "Good Low" (age 20–24 bracket) in every event; required during First Class year to commission
  • Body fat max 23% male / 34% female — not waivable
Marine option
  • PFT: plank, pull-ups or push-ups, 3-mile run — minimum 200 to activate the scholarship, 235 as a sophomore/junior
  • CFT: Movement to Contact, Ammunition Lift, Maneuver Under Fire — minimum 235
  • 265+ recommended for OCS (265 minimum, no waiver); body fat per MCO 6110.3 — not waivable

All midshipmen: qualify as Swimmer 3rd Class by the end of freshman year. Fitness failures count as aptitude deficiencies — two failures in three years starts a disenrollment package.

Source: ROD §§ 3-14, 3-17, 3-20. Official scoring references: Navy PRT Guide (MyNavyHR) · Marine Corps PFT/CFT standards (marines.mil)

How the unit is run

Midshipmen are organized as a battalion: companies of 2–4 platoons, platoons of 2–4 squads, squads of 4–10. It's run by midshipmen — a battalion commander and executive officer, operations, academic, administrative, and supply officers, plus a chief petty officer — with company and platoon staffs below them. Every midshipman must hold a position of responsibility before commissioning; leadership billets aren't reserved for seniors.

The unit's staff: the Professor of Naval Science (the unit's commanding officer), an executive officer, naval science instructors, a Marine Officer Instructor for Marine-option midshipmen, and class advisors who track your grades and degree progress.

Source: ROD §§ 3-21, 5-1, 5-2, 5-3
