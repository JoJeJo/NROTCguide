The pipeline

From midshipman to officer

How communities are assigned, what happens each summer, commissioning, and how people leave.

Service assignment

"Service assignment — not service selection." You submit your preferences. An NSTC panel weighs them against the needs of the Navy and assigns your designator. "PNSs are to communicate to students they will be assigned where the Navy needs them most."

How it works: from freshman year you log a first-choice designator in an annual survey; in your final year you rank preferences on the OPMIS Service Assignment Form — designator choices, ASTB scores (aviation), physical qualification, GPA, and aptitude. The PNS must have the "where the Navy needs you" conversation with you before your sophomore-year obligation point.

Navy-option communities: Surface Warfare, Submarines (1170), Naval Aviation — Pilot (1390) / Naval Flight Officer (1370), SEAL (1180) / EOD (1190), plus restricted-line and staff corps designators (Intelligence, Information Warfare, Cyber, Medical, Dental) as backup paths.

  • Aviation: ASTB minimums — Pilot AQR 4 / PFAR 5, NFO AQR 4 / FOFAR 5; max 3 attempts; 2.5 GPA; age 27 or under at commissioning
  • SEAL/EOD: community fitness test, clean back/knee history, recommendations from special warfare officers, Top Secret eligibility, top 25% of class
  • Nuclear: apply once calculus and physics are done, within 24 months of graduation — Naval Reactors screening is separate and doesn't guarantee a billet

Marine option: no MOS choice at NROTC — everyone is assigned their specialty at The Basic School after commissioning, except those on a Guaranteed Aviation contract. Note: a small number of Navy-option graduates commission into the Individual Ready Reserve before starting active duty.

Source: ROD §§ 4-22, 4-44, 4-45, 4-46, 4-47, 4-48, 4-49

Commissioning

To commission you need: the degree, all naval science and NROTC-specified courses, lab and leadership training, summer training (or OCS), a pre-commissioning physical, and a Secret security clearance (started within 30 days of joining the unit — no clearance, no commission). Your appointment requires a commissioning scroll approved by the Secretary of Defense, and the oath is administered by a commissioned officer on the day itself.

Source: ROD §§ 4-22, 4-51, 4-52

Disenrollment

The ROD lists 12 types of disenrollment: Academic, Inaptitude, Disciplinary, Physical Readiness, Drop on Request, Special Reasons (hardship, conscientious objection, immunization refusal, Corps of Cadets membership), Medical disqualification, Death, Dropped by the Institution, Failure to Enroll, MCRC Directed, and Not Selected for Advanced Standing.

What you owe depends on whether you're obligated, not on the type: obligated midshipmen face enlisted service or reimbursement (see The contract above). In exceptional cases — medical or extraordinary circumstances — the obligation can be waived.

Source: ROD §§ 6-15, 6-16
